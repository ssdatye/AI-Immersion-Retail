# Walmart — C-Suite Strategist Brief

*Grounded in the Customer — Retail | Role: C-Suite Strategist*
*Prepared: October 28, 2026*

> **Role prompt:** "Map CMO/CIO/COO/Head of Supply Chain/Head of Digital. What priorities show up in public reports (margin, speed, experience, shrink)?"

Source numbers in [brackets] refer to the Sources list at the end. Primary Walmart sources are preferred for citing in front of a customer.

---

## 1. The 60-Second Answer (No Technology)

Walmart's leaders are trying to grow faster and keep lowering prices while protecting thin margins. Their main tools are speed, a new mix of revenue sources (advertising, membership, marketplace), and a supply chain that runs at lower cost.

- **Pace:** "We are delivering on our strategy, powered by speed and scale." [3]
- **Price:** "We're investing in prices because customers are looking to us for value." [3]
- **Margin pressure:** One analysis found Q2 FY27 operating margin fell to 3.45%, the lowest quarterly reading in nine years, going back to fiscal 2017. [10]

---

## 2. Background: A New Leadership Team

- **New CEO:** John Furner became President and CEO of Walmart Inc. on February 1, 2026, succeeding Doug McMillon. [1]
- **New structure:** Furner: "As AI rapidly reshapes retail, we are centralizing our platforms to accelerate shared capabilities, freeing up our operating segments to be more focused on and closer to our customers and members." [1]
- **New segment heads:** David Guggina (President & CEO, Walmart U.S.), Chris Nicholas (President & CEO, Walmart International), Latriece Watkins (President & CEO, Sam's Club U.S.). [1]

**What this means:** Shared platforms now sit at the center of the company and the business units run day-to-day operations. Expect tension between the two over who owns what, especially for AI.

### Evidence for each leader

- **Furner (CEO) on speed:** "Speed is not simply a fulfillment metric; it is an acquisition strategy." [6]
- **Rainey (CFO):** Called technology-enabled productivity benefits critical to growing the omni business at lower marginal cost [11]; on the Q2 FY27 call, cited incremental margins running twice the pace of the overall business. [10]
- **Kumar (CTO):** Walmart is using AI across the business, from helping customers shop to forecasting demand, managing inventory and responding to supply-chain disruptions. Walmart uses external frontier AI models where appropriate, plus specialized models for demand forecasting and route optimization. [8]
- **Danker (AI):** At the Morgan Stanley TMT Conference (March 2026), Walmart cited four factors for being chosen by external AI platforms: assortment, price, delivery speed, and trust. [13]
- **Dallaire (Growth):** Oversees global enterprise platforms including Walmart Connect, Walmart+, Walmart Data Ventures, Vizio, and a global Marketplace platform. [1]
- **A signal of who blocks whom:** After an internal review led by Danker and Kumar, roughly 1,000 corporate employees are being cut or relocated as Walmart reorganizes its technology and AI teams. Their memo noted that "different teams [were] working on similar problems." [7]

## 4. Priorities in Public Reports

### Margin
- **Prices come first, even when margin could be kept:** Walmart received nearly $2.9B in IEEPA tariff refunds and prioritized investment in price; Walmart U.S. delivered more than 11,000 rollbacks in Q2 FY27. [3]
- **Investors pushed back:** The stock fell 9% on August 20, 2026, from $114.30 to $103.84. [12]
- **New profit sources:** Global advertising +38%; Walmart Connect in the U.S. +43% (excluding VIZIO). [3]
- **Cost pressure:** Inventory and labor are the company's two highest costs [11]; inventory reached $61.6B, up $3.9B (6.7%). [4]

### Speed
- **Growth rates:** U.S. fast delivery grew 48% in the quarter; sub-30-minute delivery expanded to 38 U.S. markets. [6]
- **Online growth is profitable:** U.S. e-commerce generated double-digit incremental margins in the first half of the year, helped by delivery density, fee-based fast delivery and automation. [14]
- **Automation scale:** 3,100 U.S. stores have some level of automated freight; more than 50% of e-commerce fulfillment volume runs through automated facilities. [14]

### Customer Experience & Loyalty
- **Sparky (AI shopping assistant):** Weekly active users up over 100% in the last quarter; customers using Sparky have an average order value about 35% higher than non-Sparky customers (Furner). [7]
- **Shopping through AI agents (Oct 21, 2026):** Walmart introduced Agent Profiles, the Walmart Agent Gateway and terms for AI agents. Walmart's returns process applies, including if an agent doesn't act as intended — linking agentic shopping directly to returns cost. [5]

### Shrink *(weakest public evidence — validate live)*
- Third-party summary data (2022–2023) puts Walmart's annual shrink losses at an estimated $3B in 2022. [16] Treat as a must-validate item, not a current fact.

## 5. Gaps Between Ambition and Reality (Worksheet A)

1. **Price investment vs. reported margin.**
   - Management points to adjusted growth: ~17% adjusted operating income growth in Q2 included a 750bps net benefit from tariff refunds. [3]
   - Reported operating income fell to $6.48B from $7.69B a year earlier. [10]
2. **Centralized AI vs. ownership by the business units.** A new structure plus a reorg of the tech teams raises the question of who owns AI value and its results. [1][7]
3. **Automation at scale is still unproven in stores.**
   - The first in-store robotic pickup system is expected to "come to life" in about six months. [9]
   - Symbotic's CEO said more sites should follow "once Walmart actually sees the system working." [9]

## 6. Bold Discovery Pack (Worksheet C)

### Provocations
- "If AI agents increasingly choose where people shop, is your moat still price and stores, or whether agents trust you? Who in this room owns that?"
- "You turned a $2.9B windfall into rollbacks and the market punished it. What is the investor case for keeping that up?"
- "If an agent orders the wrong thing, your returns policy covers it. Are returns now a cost center or a data product?"

### Discovery Questions (top 3 marked ★)
1. ★ How do the central platform teams and the segment CEOs split accountability for AI value? Who signs off on the business case?
2. ★ What is the one margin metric the CFO would trade speed for, and the one he never would?
3. ★ Where does automation stop paying off? Which stores or distribution centers have the weakest business case?
4. How does inventory growth break down between strategic stock placed for speed and misallocation?
5. How are returns and fraud tracked for orders placed through agents?
6. What is today's shrink trend, and is it still a margin lever at board level?
7. Does the 35% higher Sparky order value come from new spending or spending moved from other channels?
8. When automation increases, what happens to store labor scheduling and the associate experience?
9. Which executive is most likely to block a cross-functional AI program, and why?
