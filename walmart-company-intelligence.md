## 1. Executive Summary

**Business-first view.** Walmart is not in trouble. It is a leader under pressure. FY26 revenue was **$713.2B (+4.7%)** and Walmart kept gaining share across income bands [F, S1]. But profit is under strain:
- Reported operating income grew only **1.6%** and operating margin slipped from **4.4% to 4.2%** [F, ITL, S3].
- The core shopper is stretched.
- Costs are rising: fuel, claims, healthcare and depreciation.
- Leadership has promised that **profit will grow faster than sales** [F/S, S1, S4].

**How Walmart squares the circle** [F/I]:
1. **Keep prices lowest.** Walmart U.S. ran 11,000+ rollbacks in Q2 FY27, partly funded by a one-time ~$2.9B tariff refund [S4].
2. **Change the profit mix.** Advertising ($6.4B in FY26, +46% including VIZIO) and membership together were about **one-third of operating income** [S1, S2].
3. **Lower cost-to-serve.** Stores act as delivery nodes, distribution centers are automated, and AI-driven productivity lets **~2.1M associates support growing sales with flat headcount** [S1, S27, S30].

**Digital direction** [F]:
- AI is now positioned as the operating fabric. Furner centralized platforms in Jan 2026 because "AI rapidly reshapes retail" [S7].
- In customer-facing channels, **Sparky** (the shopping agent) is live in app, web and store, and inside ChatGPT and Gemini [S2, S13, S17].
- In stores, the associate app, digital shelf labels and computer-vision inventory tools are scaling [S20, S21].
- In the supply chain, about **3,100 U.S. stores** receive automated freight and over **50%** of e-commerce fulfillment-center volume is automated [S4].

**Execution reality** [F/I]:
- Q2 FY27 U.S. comp sales slowed to **2.6%**, the weakest in about six years [S4, S35].
- The stock fell about 9% on results day [CSS, S35].
- Most AI impact numbers are **company-reported with no baselines**.
- The one independent test of agentic checkout (ChatGPT Instant Checkout) **underperformed and was replaced** [S15, S16].
- The 2023 automation targets were **only partly met** by the FY26 deadline, and the **~20% unit-cost** target has never been reported on [S10, S24].

## 4. Strategic Alignment and Business Value

| Business value lever | Initiatives that contribute | **Demonstrated** (disclosed results) | **Projected / claimed** (not yet proven) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue growth** | Sparky, agentic channels, marketplace, fast delivery, Walmart+ | E-com +23–24% for 10+ quarters; FY26 e-com >$150B; marketplace +52%; record Walmart+ adds [F] | Sparky's *incremental* revenue; agent-channel scale; online sales reaching ~50% of total |
| **Margin** | Walmart Connect, VIZIO, Vibe, membership | Ads + membership ≈ ⅓ of operating income; U.S. e-com profitable since Q1 FY26 with double-digit incremental margins in H1 FY27 [F] | Ad incrementality; marketplace profitability |
| **Productivity** | Associate app AI, shelf labels, DC automation | Wage leverage in Q2 FY27; flat headcount on growing sales [F/S] | The ~20% unit-cost target; store-level productivity |
| **Cost-to-serve** | Fulfillment Engine, store-as-node, density, depots, drones | Rainey names density, fee-based fast delivery and "lastly, the benefits of automation" [F] | Cost per order trend; drone and depot economics |
| **Inventory performance** | AI forecasting, self-healing inventory, RFID, Scintilla | $55M rerouting savings [R] | In-stock rate and turns. **Counter-signal:** inventory +6.7% vs sales +5.9% in Q2 FY27 [F] |
| **Customer experience** | Sparky, <3-hour delivery, Sam's exit tech | 95% household reach; 23% faster club exits; record fast-delivery share [R] | Satisfaction and NPS effects (not disclosed) |

**[I] Synthesis — the Walmart flywheel:**
1. Price brings traffic.
2. Traffic and digital engagement feed advertising and membership.
3. Advertising and membership fund more price investment.
4. Automation and AI keep cost-to-serve from rising as the online mix grows.

AI matters most at two points:
- **The front door** (Sparky and agent channels), which protects traffic and advertising inventory.
- **Orchestration** (forecasting, fulfillment engine, store tools), which protects cost-to-serve.

The clearest *proven* value today is in the advertising, membership and speed metrics, not in AI-specific productivity claims.

## 6. Key Strategic Insights (Phase 1 draft)

1. **Walmart's real product is its profit mix.** Advertising and membership (≈⅓ of operating income) fund price leadership. AI matters most where it protects that engine: Sparky keeps traffic and ad inventory on Walmart's own surfaces. [F/I]
2. **Walmart rents AI distribution but keeps the customer.** After Instant Checkout converted ~3x worse, Walmart moved to Sparky-inside-ChatGPT/Gemini plus an Agent Gateway with its own terms. Cart, checkout, data and returns stay with Walmart. [F/I]
3. **Speed is the acquisition strategy, and density is the economics.** Stores as nodes, AI orchestration and fee-based fast delivery turned e-commerce profitable. Fuel (+$2B) and rising inventory are the stress points. [F/I]
4. **Automation is past peak spending but not proven on cost.** Store coverage targets were roughly met late. The unit-cost promise is undisclosed, store micro-fulfillment is a prototype, and dependence on one vendor (Symbotic) is high. [F/V]
5. **AI value is claimed more than measured.** Nearly every AI metric is self-reported and lacks a baseline. Q2 FY27 shows the margin story leaning on one-time refunds. The next 2–3 quarters test whether productivity can fund price without the windfall. [F/I]

## 8. Executive Discovery Questions and Provocations

**Provocations**
1. "Your tariff refund bought one half-year of rollbacks. When it runs out, does the next dollar of price come from advertising margin, automation savings or store labor?"
2. "If Sparky becomes the front door, every ad inside it is a tax on the answer. How much shopper trust will you trade for retail-media growth?"
3. "You put stores at the center of fulfillment and inventory now grows faster than sales. Are you buying speed with inventory turns?"

**Discovery questions**
1. Which single metric will tell you in Q4 that the H2 price investment worked?
2. Where did the 2023 automation unit-cost target land, and what is the next productivity commitment?
3. How do you separate Sparky's incremental impact from who chooses to use it?
4. How are accountability and the business case for AI value split between central platforms and the segment CEOs? [CSS]
5. What is today's cost per order by fulfillment type, and how sensitive is it to fuel?
6. How do you decide where inventory sits (store, depot or FC) when speed and turns conflict?
7. What are your returns and fraud exposure when agents buy on customers' behalf?
8. What share of store labor hours has moved from task work to fulfillment and customer-facing work since 2023?
9. How do you govern ~85% concentration on one automation vendor, and what would slow the store micro-fulfillment rollout?
10. How will you prove advertising incrementality to brands as shopping moves into AI assistants?
